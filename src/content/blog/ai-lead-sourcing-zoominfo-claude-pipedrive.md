The sales team at a food and beverage exporter was spending most of its week on work that is not selling. Searching ZoomInfo for companies that fit. Googling each one to understand what it does. Finding the right person to talk to. Typing all of it into Pipedrive. Only then could someone pick up the phone.

Now the pipeline does everything up to the call. Every lead lands in Pipedrive scored and with the research already done, the salesperson gets an alert the moment one arrives, and the first thing a human does is the call itself.

The result: **more than 40 hours saved every week for the sales team**, and a CRM that stays clean on its own.

## Where the week was going

Lead sourcing looks simple on paper. In practice, every lead went through five manual steps:

1. Search ZoomInfo for companies that match the target profile
2. Research each company on the web to confirm the fit
3. Find the decision makers, not just any contact
4. Create the organization, the people and the deal in Pipedrive
5. Remember to follow up

Multiply that by every lead, every week, and the team had little time left for the conversations that actually close deals. Worse, the CRM slowly filled with duplicates and accounts nobody ever called.

## How the pipeline works

### 1. Sourcing from ZoomInfo

The pipeline pulls companies from ZoomInfo that match the team's target profile. This replaces hours of manual searching with a steady, consistent flow of candidates.

### 2. Web enrichment

ZoomInfo data is a starting point, not the full picture. For each company the pipeline researches the web to fill in what the database misses: what the company actually does, what it sells and whether it is a real fit.

### 3. Scoring and decision makers with Claude

Claude reads everything gathered so far and does two jobs a person used to do by hand. It judges how well the company matches the team's criteria, and it identifies the people who make the buying decision. Every lead keeps its score, so the team knows at a glance where to spend its attention.

### 4. Automatic CRM records in Pipedrive

Every lead is written into Pipedrive with its score, the organization, the decision makers and the research attached. The ones that fit become the team's priority, and the ones that do not stay on record for later instead of being lost. Nobody types anything. When the salesperson opens the record, the context for the first call is already there.

### 5. Instant alert, then a human call

The moment a new qualified lead enters the pipeline, the salesperson gets an alert and makes the first call. This is deliberate. The pipeline removes the busywork, and the relationship stays with a person.

## The part most pipelines skip: keeping the CRM clean

Adding leads is the easy half. A pipeline that only adds will bury the team in duplicates and dead accounts within months. Three mechanisms keep this one healthy.

**Re-processing forgotten accounts.** The system searches the organizations already in Pipedrive, finds the ones that were never contacted, enriches them again and sends them back into the pipeline. Leads the team paid for and forgot about get a second chance.

**Duplicate detection.** Every new record is checked against what already exists. Duplicates are caught and removed, so the same company never gets called twice by two different reps.

**Failure alerts.** If any step fails, a source times out or a record cannot be written, the team gets an email. A silent failure in a sales pipeline means leads quietly stop arriving, which is the worst kind of bug because nobody notices until the quarter is lost.

## What changed

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Finding leads | Manual searches in ZoomInfo | Continuous sourcing from ZoomInfo |
| Research | Googling each company | Automatic web enrichment |
| Decision makers | Found by hand | Identified by Claude |
| CRM entry | Typed into Pipedrive | Written automatically, research attached |
| First contact | Whenever someone remembered | Salesperson alerted instantly |
| CRM hygiene | Duplicates and forgotten accounts | Deduplicated, forgotten accounts re-processed |

**40+ hours a week back for the sales team.** That is roughly a full-time person's worth of work, redirected from data entry to selling.

## What I learned building it

**Automate up to the human moment, not past it.** The pipeline hands over to a person at the right moment. The first call is where trust starts, and a person makes it with all the context ready.

**Score everything, lose nothing.** Every lead goes into the CRM with a score, so the team works the best ones first and the rest stay available. A company that does not fit today may fit next quarter, and the re-processing step brings it back when it does.

**Hygiene is a feature.** Deduplication, re-processing and failure alerts are not glamorous, but they are the difference between a system the team trusts in month six and one they quietly stop using.

## When this makes sense for your team

A pipeline like this pays off when:

- Your salespeople spend more time researching and typing than talking to prospects
- You already pay for a data source like ZoomInfo or Apollo but use a fraction of it
- Your CRM has duplicates, stale accounts or leads nobody followed up on
- You want AI to do the research, not to replace the conversation

If your team only works a handful of leads a month, a good manual routine is enough. If they work dozens a week, the hours add up fast.
